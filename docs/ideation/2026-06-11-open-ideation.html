Codebase Context

Tamper Signal (v1.5.1) puts cryptographically signed receipts on each stage of a data pipeline: input-hash, code-hash, output-hash, plus human-readable control totals (row counts, sums, date ranges, null counts). Verification collapses the chain to a traffic light: green intact, yellow caveats, red broken at a named link with a totals delta. Files on disk, no database, no server. The honest claim is continuity, not correctness: it can prove nobody changed the data, not that the data was right.

What ships today

  • Two stacks, interchangeable chains. Python (receipts CLI + receipt_step) and npm (tamper-signal CLI + receiptStep/loadCsv/rebuildChain/canonicalDocument/verifyChain), byte-identical via golden vectors. Ed25519, JCS canonicalization.
  • Browser surfaces re-verify in the viewer's browser via Web Crypto, no build step: badge, inline status light (with metric flagging via data-receipt-column), verified data tab, inspectable console, React wrapper, web component.
  • Framework attach helpers: Flask, FastAPI, Express, Streamlit. CLI exit codes are the light. --json structured verdict. Key rotation, CI signing via TAMPER_SIGNAL_KEY, Sigstore transparency-log anchoring.
  • Site: on-site docs, blog, live demo at tampersignal.com. Zero open issues.

Leverage points the grounding surfaced

  • The crypto core is hardened; the adoption and observability surface is thin. Recent PRs (#24–#31) are all presentation/distribution.
  • Receipts accumulate every run, but the history is untapped — no diff, no continuity-over-time, no drift view.
  • The actual vibe-coding surface (notebooks, AI assistants) has zero integration — every transform is hand-wrapped.
  • Verify is a sameness check, but real reporting data legitimately drifts as a period matures (late-arriving conversions, attribution backfill, more of the window accruing) — so strict hash identity is the wrong question for scheduled refreshes (hence --warn-drift ships off by default).
  • External tailwinds: vibe-coding trust gap (AI code ~1.7× more logic errors), EU AI Act Article 10 data-provenance mandate in force Aug 2026, Sigstore now mainstream, C2PA-style file-native provenance rising. Closest cousin (PipeRider/Recce) is warehouse-bound; the file-native cryptographic niche is unoccupied.

1

Period-over-period continuity: memory, drift bands, and real anomaly callouts

lead push
basis: direct + reasoned confidence 90% complexity Medium–High
Description

Real-world reporting data is supposed to move. A paid-media export, a social post's stats, or a Google Analytics pull all drift day over day and week over week as the reporting window matures: late conversions land, attribution backfills, more of the period accrues. Differences are normal, so the useful question is never "is this byte-for-byte identical to last time?" It is "did things stay on task / within a sensible threshold over time, and is anything a genuine anomaly?"

This is the original ideas 1 and 2 fused into one push, because neither is complete alone:

a. A memory (history & diff)

receipts diff runA/chain.json runB/chain.json compares two runs and names what moved — which stage's code or hash changed, and the control-totals delta (rows, sums, null spikes). A receipts log view extends that into a trend across days, weeks, and quarters. The data is already on disk; only the comparison view is missing.

b. A sense of normal (drift bands)

Let the producer bind expected ranges to control totals at sign time (row_count 10,000 ±2%, revenue within bounds, null-rate <1%). Refreshes that drift within the declared band stay green; only true outliers trip yellow ("a human should look"). Together, the band defines normal and the history shows whether each refresh tracked it.

Basis

direct: receipts already store control totals per run, yet the history is unused and no diff primitive exists in tamper_signal or node; and --warn-drift already ships off by default precisely because filters, aggregations, and maturing data legitimately move totals — the product already knows this is the hard problem and currently punts it to a binary flag.

reasoned: for scheduled refreshes, byte-identity is always false and therefore useless; the signal that actually carries information is "stayed within the band I declared, tracked over time." Five of six ideation frames converged on the history half, and the design-basis (engineering tolerance) analogy supplied the band half.

Rationale

The persona refreshes social, paid-media, and analytics exports where data maturation is the norm. A trust tool that fires on every normal day-over-day change gets muted, and a muted trust tool is worthless — so teaching Tamper Signal what normal drift looks like, and reserving the light for genuine anomalies, is what keeps the green/yellow/red verdict credible enough to leave switched on.

It is also the highest-leverage single build: the diff/history is the keystone that unlocks log, drift-over-time, and bisect-which-run-broke-it for free, while the bands are what make those views meaningful for recurring uploads. This is the user-endorsed lead push.

Downsides

Two mechanisms is more scope than one, so it needs a tight MVP — likely diff plus a simple declared band on row_count and key sums first, the longitudinal log second. Bands edge toward "correctness" and must be fenced so they remain the producer's declared expectation, never a claim the data is right. Comparing and trending runs needs a stable run-identity / retention convention the tool does not define yet.

2

Agent-native MCP server: the AI verifies its own data work

basis: direct + external confidence 80% complexity Medium
Description

Ship Tamper Signal as an MCP server exposing receipt_step and verify_chain as agent tools, plus a drop-in instruction snippet. The AI assistant that writes a transform also wraps and signs it; before it publishes a dashboard or hands numbers downstream, it calls verify and self-gates — refuse on red, escalate to a human on yellow. Instrumentation and verification stop being a human afterthought and become the path of least resistance for the agent already doing the work.

Basis

direct: --json already emits a structured verdict "for CI and coding agents," and AGENTS.md is written as an agent runbook ("Pointing a coding agent at this repo?"). The gating primitive exists; the MCP surface is the missing wrapper. external: MCP is the mainstream way agents call tools, and the central tailwind is that AI-generated pipelines are the fastest-growing source of silent failure.

Rationale

The persona's core failure mode is an AI assistant quietly producing wrong numbers. Letting the agent verify its own output before a human ever sees it closes the loop at the exact point of failure, and it matches this repo's existing agent-native-parity ethos. Three frames converged on it.

Downsides

An agent self-attesting is weaker than an independent check — it proves "I signed what I produced," not that the agent's logic was sound. Needs framing so it complements, not replaces, human/CI verification.

3

Zero-config auto-instrumentation: notebook magic + pandas shim

basis: direct + external confidence 70% complexity Medium
Description

Meet the vibe-coder where the work happens. A %%receipt Jupyter/Colab cell magic treats each marked cell as a stage, hashing cell source + the DataFrame it produces, no decorator and no chain_dir argument. And an import tamper_signal.auto shim monkeypatches the common pandas I/O boundaries (read_excel/read_csv → ingest; to_excel/to_csv → output receipt) so an AI-generated script produces a chain from a single pasted import line.

Basis

direct: receipt_step already supports pandas DataFrames, so the canonicalization is solved — only the auto-capture wrapper is new; grounding names the notebook/AI surface as having zero integration. external: Ruff, pre-commit, and Elementary all won by driving setup to near zero (one hook, one package line, instant local feedback).

Rationale

Hand-wrapping every stage is the single biggest adoption tax; a six-stage pipeline is six manual edits today, and the users who most need this adopted Tamper Signal because their AI-generated pipeline is a black box to them. Collapsing "install → wrap each transform" into "install → one import" is the difference between a tool people try and one they keep.

Downsides

Notebooks re-run cells out of order, which makes receipt semantics genuinely tricky (what is "the chain" when cell 3 ran twice?). Monkeypatching pandas I/O is fragile across versions and can surprise users. Both need careful, honest scoping.

4

Consumer trust layer: verify-by-URL, verify-a-stranger, trust-on-first-use

basis: direct + reasoned confidence 68% complexity Medium
Description

Reframe the consumer of the verdict from the pipeline owner to the dashboard viewer — the boss, client, or auditor who actually needs to trust the number. Three moves compound: receipts inspect for checking data you didn't produce (surface the signer identity, no pre-shared key required); a trust-on-first-use signer keybook (SSH known_hosts model) that pins a signer and loudly flags a key substitution; and receipts publish → a shareable tampersignal.com/v/<hash> link that re-verifies live in the recipient's own browser.

Basis

direct: Sigstore anchoring already attaches an OIDC identity to a chain, and tampersignal.com already re-verifies a committed chain in-browser — verify-by-URL is the live demo generalized. reasoned: nothing architecturally requires the verifier to be the producer; the receipt is self-contained, so the producer-only verification surface is a UX assumption, not a cryptographic one.

Rationale

For an agency handing a dashboard to a client, the highest-value moment isn't "I know my pipeline is clean" — it's "my client can prove it themselves," without a key exchange or a call back to me. That is the wedge from "tool I run on my own pipeline" to "trust mark a market shares," and it feeds the badge growth loop.

Downsides

Hosted verify-by-URL adds an (optional) service surface that must keep verification client-side to preserve the no-server-in-the-trust-path moat. Identity/trust UX is easy to get subtly wrong (the whole point is not crying wolf or rubber-stamping).

5

Distribution flywheel: official GitHub Action + README status badge

basis: external + direct confidence 85% complexity Low
Description

Package the verify step as a one-line marketplace Action (welovejeff/tamper-signal-verify@v1) that sets the check status from the exit code and posts the run-over-run totals diff (idea 1) as a PR comment. Pair it with a drop-in README badge — generated locally or in CI from the --json verdict, no server — that renders the latest verdict or continuity score: "pipeline intact · 142-run streak." The badge is the only feature whose value compounds per user rather than per run.

Basis

external: the Ruff / pre-commit / Elementary adoption arc is explicit in the grounding — install → local feedback → CI step → README badge as social proof that recruits the next team. direct: exit codes already are the light, --json ships, and CI signing exists, so the primitives are all there; what's missing is the packaged Action and badge, and recent momentum is already on distribution.

Rationale

This is the cheapest high-certainty win on the list and the natural next step after the docs/blog push: it completes the adoption arc and makes a broken chain un-mergeable rather than something a human hopes to check. Every README badge is a passive ad that pulls in the next user.

Downsides

A marketplace Action commits the project to a versioned public CI contract to maintain. A badge that can show green on a stale chain would be worse than none — it has to be honest about staleness.

6

Compliance & interop substrate: in-toto/DSSE export + EU AI Act evidence pack

basis: external + direct confidence 62% complexity High
Description

Treat "continuity, not correctness" as the moat, not a disclaimer: position Tamper Signal as the lightweight, file-native data-pipeline complement to SLSA/in-toto (which cover software supply chain). Add an export mode that emits each receipt as a DSSE-wrapped in-toto attestation so existing Sigstore/SLSA/policy tooling can consume it, plus a one-command "evidence pack" (chain + public keys + anchors + a human-readable PDF/JSON of the verdict and control-total deltas) framed as an EU AI Act Article 10 record-keeping artifact.

Basis

external: in-toto and DSSE are established open standards with mainstream SLSA verifier tooling; EU AI Act Article 10 (in force Aug 2026) mandates documenting data origin, processing steps, and quality measures — a receipt chain is exactly that artifact, and no lightweight tool fills the role. direct: Sigstore anchoring already ships and the --json verdict is the seed of the structured export.

Rationale

Adopting a standard envelope is a single decision that plugs receipts into a large pre-existing universe of consumers (transparency logs, policy engines, auditors) you never have to build for. It routes the product toward compliance buyers who have budget and a dated mandate, and gives a crisp category ("SLSA for your data pipeline") without educating the market from scratch.

Downsides

The heaviest item here and the furthest from today's persona — risks pulling the product toward enterprise ceremony before the small-team loop is fully won. Best treated as a strategic bet to validate (a candidate for ce-strategy) rather than the immediate next build.
