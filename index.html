how it works

Receipts link. Links verify. Breaks locate.

Each receipt records the SHA-256 of its input, its code, and its output, plus human-legible control totals. Receipts link because each stage's input hash must equal the previous stage's output hash. Hashes say "broken"; totals say "how broken."

export.xlsx │ ▼ [ingest] ─────────────▶ 000_source.json evidence hash + semantic hash + totals, signed │ ▼ [transform_clean] ────▶ 001_clean.json input hash == previous output hash │ ▼ [transform_agg] ──────▶ 002_aggregate.json │ ▼ dashboard data ◀────── receipts verify: walk every link, check every signature

What this proves, and what it doesn't

This proves continuity, not correctness. The chain shows your dashboard numbers descend from the ingested export through a known sequence of code, and it locates the exact stage where a number changed unexpectedly. If the source export is itself wrong, the chain faithfully verifies wrong numbers. It is not a data-quality tool.

Also worth knowing: the signing key lives on your machine, and today that local keypair is the whole root of trust. External anchoring (Sigstore, RFC 3161) is on the roadmap to close that gap. Tamper-evident, not tamper-proof: we detect, we don't prevent.
